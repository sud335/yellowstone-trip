Use these as optional layers, not a fourth itinerary. The marked priority stops are worth protecting. Everything else is a bonus when parking and timing cooperate.

When the day changes

Choose a backup plan

Select the situation that best matches the morning. These swaps protect the overnight destination and the most important sights.

Protect the anchors

Cut stops in a fixed order

  1. Sept 21: Skip Tower Fall and Lookout Point first. If departure slips past 6:30 AM, drop Lamar and start with Artist Point, then Hayden Valley.
  2. Sept 22: Skip Lewis Falls and North Jenny Lake Road. Keep West Thumb, Oxbow Bend, Jenny Lake, and the Tetonia arrival.
  3. Sept 23: Skip Gibbon Falls first, then shorten Norris to Porcelain Basin. Keep Mammoth, the North Entrance, and the overnight transfer.

Use clouds strategically

Save mountain views for the clearest window

  1. Foggy Sept 20: Keep Fountain Paint Pot first and move Grand Prismatic later. Low steam and fog can hide the spring’s color.
  2. Cloudy Sept 22: Continue through West Thumb and the park. Pause at Jackson Lake Dam or Oxbow only when the peaks appear, then protect the easy Jenny Lake walk.
  3. Wet Sept 23: Use rain shells at Norris and Mammoth. Skip exposed bonus viewpoints, but keep the commercial soak if roads remain safe.

Never force a closed route

Use the outside-park escape routes

  1. Dunraven closure on Sept 21: Drop Lamar. Reach Canyon and Hayden through Norris, then return the same way.
  2. South Entrance closure on Sept 22: Leave Yellowstone through West Yellowstone and use Island Park and Ashton to reach Tetonia. Skip the Tetons instead of gambling with daylight.
  3. Norris-to-Mammoth closure on Sept 23: From West Yellowstone, use US 191 north toward Bozeman, I-90 east to Livingston, then US 89 south to Pray. Drop the soak.

Optional aerial stop

Fly outside the national parks

Best candidate: non-Wilderness Caribou-Targhee National Forest land in the Big Hole Mountains west of Tetonia, with the Packsaddle Lake access area as a starting point for your same-day check.

This can fit after checking into Tetonia on Sept 22 if daylight, wind, road conditions, and the family’s energy are all good. Treat it as a candidate, not a pre-cleared launch site. The exact takeoff pin must be verified in the Forest Service map and an FAA-approved B4UFLY service immediately before flight.

View candidate area ↗ Check B4UFLY options ↗

Non-negotiable rules

  • No launch, landing, or operation inside Yellowstone or Grand Teton National Park.
  • Do not launch from designated Wilderness, a developed recreation site, private land without permission, or an area covered by a temporary flight restriction.
  • Complete TRUST and carry proof. Register drones weighing 250 g or more and meet Remote ID requirements when applicable.
  • Stay at or below 400 feet in uncontrolled airspace, keep visual line of sight, yield to aircraft, and avoid people and wildlife.
  • Wind in Teton Valley can change quickly. Skip the flight if gusts or visibility are marginal.
